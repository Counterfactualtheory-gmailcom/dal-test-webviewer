COMPAiSS Research Library

Research & Publications

Research on authorization-first AI governance, execution-gated generation, institutional evidence, hallucination risk, enterprise RAG, regulatory alignment, empirical evaluation, and the design of governed AI systems for regulated institutions.

COMPAiSS research examines a central question for regulated institutions: how should artificial intelligence operate when the authority to provide an answer matters as much as the factual plausibility of the answer itself?

The research program examines the architectural, empirical, regulatory, operational, and economic implications of governing institutional AI before primary inference occurs rather than relying primarily on controls applied during or after generation.

This library consolidates COMPAiSS research published in both HTML and PDF formats, including major research papers, architectural analyses, empirical evaluations, governance reports, regulatory assessments, technical figures, and supporting research materials.

Research focus: COMPAiSS distinguishes factual correctness from institutional authority. Its research asks not only whether an AI system can produce a plausible answer, but whether the institution has authorized the evidence and conditions under which that answer may be generated.
